### The findings that shape the rest

1. **The Skippy item runs its own gameplay logic and publishes it as quest facts.** `Items.Preset_Yukimura_Skippy` has an `OnAttach` package that toggles `mq007_skippy_shoot`, `mq007_skippy_reload` and `mq007_skippy_aiming` while V holds it, and the mode flag `mq007_skippy_aim_at_head` re-weights its smart targeting. Facts can be listened to without any polling (`QuestsSystem.RegisterEntity`).
2. **The game keeps a gameplay fact bus** written by `DataTrackingSystem`: `gmpl_npc_killed_by_player`, `gmpl_npc_defeated_by_player`, `gmpl_npc_incapacitated_by_player`, `gmpl_npc_finished_by_player`, `gmpl_npc_downed_by_player` (pulses: set to 1, reset to 0 after exactly 1.00 s), cumulative counters `gmpl_npcs_killed_by_player` and siblings, `gmpl_player_range_attack(s)` (one per shot), `gmpl_player_melee_attack(s)`, `gmpl_player_quickhack(s)`. Skippy's own quest graph consumes the kill and defeat pulses. They exclude civilians and crowd (see 2.1).
3. **A once-per-kill hook with the whole damage history exists** (`DataTrackingSystem.OnNPCKillDataTrackingRequest`) and is what community kill-counter mods wrap.
4. **The vanilla Skippy already barks in combat** (19 lines, 20 to 45 s pacing). The add-on must decide to share or silence them (1.4).

**Best no-poll signal (new finding).** Skippy's own `OnAttach` package toggles three quest facts while the pistol is the attached weapon (section 1.3): `mq007_skippy_shoot`, `mq007_skippy_reload`, `mq007_skippy_aiming`. They are exactly "Skippy is in V's hand and V is firing, reloading, aiming". ### 1.3 The two personalities

**What the game stores.** Four facts decide everything. They are quest facts, readable with `Game.GetQuestsSystem():GetFactStr(name)` (the repo does this in `mod/relationship-state.lua:29` and `mod/world-knowledge.lua:115`). Caveat: a missing fact also reads 0.

| Fact | Meaning | Set where | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `mq007_skippy_aim_at_head` | **1 = Stone Cold Killer** (smart targeting weights the head: head tracking +5, legs +1), **0 or unset = Puppy-Loving Pacifist** (legs +5, head +1). The item itself reads this fact: the `OnAttach` package applies the head-weighted stat group on the weapon only while the fact equals 1 and the actor is the player. Community notes flip it with `Game.SetDebugFact("mq007_skippy_aim_at_head", 1 or 0)`. | Set to 1 in pickup scene `mq007_01_gun_found` when V chooses "Head" over "Knees"; set to 0 in `mq007_03` when V picks "ChangeSetting"; set back to 1 in another `mq007_03` branch after the non-lethal path. | VERIFIED (tweak dump and scene graphs); the SetDebugFact tip is a 2022 forum claim, UNVERIFIED |
| `mq007_opt_A` | 1 if V picked "Head" at pickup. | `mq007_01` | VERIFIED |
| `mq007_skippy_turned_pacifist` | 1 once Skippy locks himself into pacifist mode for good. `mq007_04_skippy_return` branches on it (>= 1 gives the "Puppy-Loving Pacifist" diagnostic and Regina resets him to factory settings; otherwise the "Stone Cold Killer" diagnostic). Nothing resets it. | `mq007_03`, followed by a write of `aim_at_head = 0` | VERIFIED |
| `mq007_skippy_goes_emo` | Alternate-personality switch: when 1 the vanilla bark graph plays the `SkippyNoReturnOneliner` entry of scene `mq007_04` instead of the combat barks of scene `mq007_02`. | read by `mq007_gameplay_comments`; writer not located (the facts list shows it exists) | read VERIFIED, writer UNVERIFIED |
| `mq007_skippy_shoot`, `mq007_skippy_reload`, `mq007_skippy_aiming` | **Live weapon-activity facts, switched by the item itself** through `Effectors.ToggleFactEffector` while Skippy is attached: on while the player's ranged-weapon state machine is in `Shoot`, in `Reload`, and the upper-body state is `Aim`, off when the state ends. The vanilla bark graph reads `reload` and `aiming`. | the item's `OnAttach` package | VERIFIED (tweak dump); exact on/off semantics of `ToggleFactEffector` UNVERIFIED |
| `yukimura_skippy_owned` | 1 once the pistol has been looted. | the item's `OnLooted` | VERIFIED |

**How the game moves between modes.** While Skippy is equipped, an in-place phase inside `mq007_gameplay_comments` counts what V does with him: `gmpl_npc_killed_by_player` pulses (set to 1 by the game's `DataTrackingSystem` and reset to 0 by the game 1.00 s later, see 2.1) add 1 to `mq007_skippy_enemies_lethal` (and to `mq007_skippy_enemies_total`); `gmpl_npc_defeated_by_player`, `gmpl_npc_incapacitated_by_player` and `gmpl_npc_downed_by_player` pulses add 1 to `mq007_skippy_enemies_non_lethal`. At 50 non-lethal the fact `mq007_skippy_50_enemies_maimed` is raised; at 50 lethal with `mq007_opt_A` set, `mq007_skippy_50_enemies_killed_and_stoneColdKiller` is raised. Either (an exclusive-or) sets `mq007_skippy_50_enemies_defeated`, which starts scene `mq007_03_skippy_about_killing` ("Skippy talks about what V has been doing") and equips and locks the gun during the talk (restrictions `GameplayRestriction.FirearmsNoUnequipNoSwitch`, `NoPhone`, `NoWorldInteractions`, `PhoneCall`). The conversation's choice then decides which way the flag swings: the scene starts with a branch on `mq007_skippy_50_enemies_killed_and_stoneColdKiller`. On one side a choice captioned "ChangeSetting" (against "ParentalControl") writes `turned_pacifist = 1` and `aim_at_head = 0`; on the other a three-way reply (captions "Bad", "Hm", "Joke") leads to a write of `aim_at_head = 1`. Which branch is which was read from graph edges and option captions, not played, so treat the branch assignment as inferred. The community summary agrees (the mode swaps to the other after 50 neutralisations): [Cyberpunk Wiki, Skippy](https://cyberpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Skippy) (could not be fetched here, read from a search snippet).

**What the mode does in play.** The mode is a smart-targeting weighting, not a hard rule: Killer weights the head (+5), Pacifist weights the legs (+5), and the other body zones stay at +1 in both. So in Pacifist mode the lock-on aims at limbs and enemies are usually downed or incapacitated, in Killer mode shots go for the head and kill. V can still aim by hand and kill with Skippy in either mode, and the quest counters (below) decide how Skippy reacts afterwards. For the add-on the persistent, readable difference is the fact value, plus whether neutralisation pulses are lethal or not.

**Reading the mode.** Lua: `local pacifist = Game.GetQuestsSystem():GetFactStr('mq007_skippy_turned_pacifist') >= 1`, `local head = Game.GetQuestsSystem():GetFactStr('mq007_skippy_aim_at_head') >= 1`. Mode = `head and 'killer' or 'pacifist'`; `turned_pacifist` is the locked flavour. Read on demand (when drawing the gun, at combat start, when building a remark), never on a timer. To react to a change without polling use `QuestsSystem.RegisterEntity(factName, entityID)` (VERIFIED in the old dump and 2.31 bytecode: `delamainTaxiComponent` uses it; the entity then receives a `FactChangedEvent` with `GetFactName()`); it needs a redscript `event` handler, CET cannot receive it directly.

### 1.4 What the vanilla Skippy already says in combat

The vanilla barks matter because an AI add-on that ignores them will talk over, or double, the game. They come from `base\quest\minor_quests\mq007\phases\mq007_gameplay_comments.questphase` (graph read in full; it runs for the whole game once V owns the gun) which plays scene `mq007_02_skippy_reactions` through these entry points: `SkippyTaunts01-05` (5), `SkippyOnGore01-05` (5), `Autofire` (3), `FireDelay` (2), `OnReload` (3), `old` (1, unused entry) = 19 lines, 2.0 to 7.7 s each (about 4.8 s mean). The graph's gates:

- Runs only while the equipped-item condition holds, scene tier is `Tier1_FullGameplay` and content is not blocked (`questContentCondition ... isContentBlocked`).
- Pauses: a 20 s wait after the equip/combat gate before the first opportunity, 30 s and 45 s waits in the reload and aiming loops, 250 ms / 500 ms / 2 s debounces. Randomizers use `IgnoreLastUsed` or `IgnoreAllUsed`, so lines do not repeat until the set is used.
- Kill reactions (`SkippyOnGore`, `SkippyTaunts`) are driven by the facts `gmpl_npc_killed_by_player` / `...defeated...` / `...incapacitated...` / `...downed...`, each polled by the quest every 100 ms (so vanilla reacts within about 0.1 s of the game setting the fact).
- `OnReload` is driven by the fact `mq007_skippy_reload`; `Autofire` and `FireDelay` follow `mq007_skippy_aiming` and a forced `questUseWeapon` shoot node (Skippy fires by himself as a gag), only when V is not in an upper-body Aim state and not in a vehicle.
- A knockdown (status effects `BaseStatusEffect.BarbedWireKnockdown`, `BikeKnockdown`, `HitReactionKnockdown`) or the fact `mq007_interrupt_skippy_dialogue` interrupts a running Skippy scene.
- Scene lines are `Vo_Expression_Spoken`, custom Wwise routing `vo_3d_important`, visual style `invisible` (no overhead caption), i.e. 3D voice from the gun with no subtitle bubble.

Design consequence: the add-on either coexists (AI remarks only on events the vanilla set does not cover: combat start and end, low health, vehicle, heat, civilians; and a long pause after a vanilla bark), or replaces the kill barks, which needs a way to silence the vanilla graph. Candidates (all UNVERIFIED, test live): set the fact `mq007_skippy_goes_emo` (diverts to a different line set, so not silence); keep the fact `mq007_interrupt_skippy_dialogue` high (its reading in `mq007_03` shows it stops a Skippy scene, its effect on the bark phase is not shown); or stop the running scene through the scene system. Never edit the quest files; ship nothing that replaces `final.redscripts` content outside our own r6 scripts.

### 1.4.1 The text beside the gun (the "hologram"), and why our remarks cannot use it (2026-10-14)

Live report: the game shows Skippy's own combat lines as text in a small hologram next to the gun, not in the subtitle bar. Findings, read on 2026-10-14 from the 2.31 script dump (Codeberg, as above), the exported quest phases and the exported localization:

- **The script subtitle pipeline never shows these lines.** VERIFIED. Every reaction line is type `Invisible` (our log: `speaker LocKey#46444, type Invisible`). `BaseSubtitlesGameController.IsMainDialogLine` excludes `Invisible` (`cyberpunk/UI/subtitles/baseSubtitlesControllers.swift:532`). `SubtitlesGameController.ShouldDisplayLine` shows only main lines plus two always-visible types (`subtitlesControllers.swift:53-58`). `ChattersGameController.ShouldDisplayLine` shows only OverHead, OverHeadAlwaysVisible, Radio and GlobalTV (`chattersControllers.swift:176-207`). No other `BaseSubtitlesGameController` subclass exists in the files read.
- **The hologram text is a fixed, pre-written string looked up by the voice line's id.** VERIFIED. The game's onscreens table (`base\localization\en-us\onscreens\onscreens.json`) has exactly 19 `vo_onscreens` entries, all in mq007, with keys `Story-base-quest-minor_quests-mq007-vo_onscreens-<16 hex>`. Example: primaryKey 80189, "Provocation. Taste my lead, bitch!", key suffix `19B764D888405000`. Each hex is the string id of one reaction line in `mq007_02_skippy_reactions`: `0x19B764D888405000 = 1853060652847878144` is that line's `sid` in `scene-lines.jsonl`. No other `Invisible` line in the game (the Arasaka AI, the hallucination voices) has such an entry. The reaction lines also carry `isHolocallSpeaker = 1`. So whatever draws the hologram takes the line's string id and shows the onscreen string stored for that id. It does not read the text the line carries.
- **The scriptable path to the quest's Skippy prop carries animation names only.** VERIFIED. `mq007.questphase` attaches `#mq007_01_skippy_prop` to V's `WeaponRight` slot and drives it with `CustomUIAnimationEvent` names: `skippy_scene_start`/`stop`, the faces `skippy_grinn`, `sad`, `scared`, `happy` and `anger`, and `skippy_transition`. It also plays `mq007_skippy_hologram_start`, `mq007_skippy_hologram_end` and `mq007_skippy_hologram_moving_expressions` on the prop. The combat phase (`phases/mq007_gameplay_comments`) sends only `skippy_scene_stop`. `CustomAnimationsGameController` and `SceneScreenGameController` play a named ink animation and have no text field (`cyberpunk/UI/common/customAnimationsGameController.swift:61-70`, `cyberpunk/devices/UI/tv/sceneScreenGameController.swift`).
- **The consumer is not in the scripts.** UNVERIFIED (not found). No script reads `vo_onscreens`. The widget that draws the text is native, or sits in an ink or entity resource that is not exported here (the gun's `.ent` and its ink library). About 700 dump files under `devices/` were not fetched (rate limit).

**Conclusion:** our AI text cannot be put into that hologram. Its text comes from a localization table keyed by the voice line's id, so a new sentence has no entry to show. Entries can only be added when the game loads, from files, not while it runs. Nothing scriptable passes a string to that widget. The remarks keep the current subtitle (the dialogue line "Skippy (AI)", `mod/subtitles.lua`). A side effect to watch live: when "Replace the game's own lines" mutes a game line (voice stopped, subtitle filtered), the hologram may still show that line's text, because the mute does not reach it.

**Audio:** the game's lines play on the scene system's Wwise route (`vo_3d_important`). Scripts can only start Wwise events on an entity (`GameObject.PlaySoundEvent`, `AudioSystem.Play` and `PlayOnEmitter`, VERIFIED in `core/entity/gameObject.swift` and `core/systems/audioSystem.swift`); none of them plays generated PCM, so our voice cannot use that route. Instead, Skippy's speaker now has `voiceFrom` (his active weapon; `docs/ADDON_API.md`). The browser voice takes its pan and distance from the gun in V's hand, not from V's body, and falls back to V if the gun is not within 3 m of the camera. The native Omni phrases still play on V.

**Next steps if the hologram is still wanted:** (1) Open the Skippy weapon's `.ent` and its ink library in WolvenKit to see whether a text widget exists and what controller class feeds it. (2) Add a read-only CET probe on `GameObject.OnCustomUIAnimationEvent` (event-driven) to log which entity fires during a combat bark. Neither step is needed for the current fallback.

**Recommended shape.** Listeners and wraps live in one small redscript file that does nothing but bump integers and set fields in a collector object (`@addField` on `PlayerPuppet`), keeping a ring of the last N kill records. Lua never sees an event individually; it drains the collector with one getter call on a 0.25 to 0.5 s tick that exists only while combat is on (the pattern of `mod/battle-observer.lua`'s half-second policy poll), or on a priority event pushed once through a flag the tick reads. This keeps the per-event work in compiled script and the Lua work at two calls per second. Where an event is rare (combat state, heat, district, defeat), a direct CET `Observe` is simpler and equally cheap.

**Two traps.** (1) `DamageSystem` runs the whole pipeline (`PreProcess`, `Process`, `PostProcess`) also for aim-preview hits: `gameProjectedHitEvent extends gameHitEvent` with `projectionPipeline = true` (`damageSystem.swift:34`). Any wrap of `ProcessLocalizedDamage`, `PreProcess` or `Process` must return early when `hitEvent.projectionPipeline` is true, or it fires while V merely aims. `OnDamageDealt`, `OnDamageReceived`, `DamagePipelineFinalized` and the `RegisterListener` callbacks are the real-damage path. (2) A shotgun blast delivers several hit events in one frame; the one published CET mod that hooks `OnDamageInflicted` (Night City Haptics) debounces it to 80 ms. Treat every per-hit hook as 10 to 30 calls per second at worst and gate it.

Consequences for the add-on, all UNVERIFIED in play but read straight from the code:

1. **Use the counters for a per-event signal, not the pulses.** A pulse set to 1 while already 1 (two kills inside one second) is a same-value write, and whether a listener fires for it is the first probe. The counters change on every record, so `gmpl_npcs_killed_by_player` (and `..._downed_...`) is a reliable "one more neutralisation" edge, and the delta between two reads is the number of kills since the last read.
2. **The pulses are one second wide.** The vanilla Skippy graph polls them every 100 ms (section 1.4) and works because of that. A listener gets the rise immediately and the fall one second later (ignore the fall).
3. **Civilians and crowd never appear.** `NPCPuppet.SendDataTrackingEvent` returns early for `IsCharacterCivilian()` and `IsCrowd()`, so these facts do not count them. Civilian casualties need the death hook (2.2, row i).
4. **They count V's neutralisations, not companion kills.** `IsSourcePlayer(attackData)` is checked for the achievement counters; whether a companion's kill still raises the fact depends on who sends the request and must be probed (probe 11). Kill attribution to Skippy needs the weapon id (2.3).
5. **No Skippy gating.** The bus is global. Attribution to the gun (only count it when `Items.mq007_skippy` is the weapon) is done in the add-on.

### 2.3 What one kill record can hold (a single callback, zero polling)

Inside one wrap of `NPCPuppet.OnDeath` or a `RewardKiller` wrap, with the last damage-listener hit kept in a field, the add-on can build this record at once: `ts` (game clock stamp from Lua), `killer` (V, a companion, other), `weaponItem` (compare with `Items.mq007_skippy`), `attackType`, `headshot`, `critical`, `nonlethal`, `stealthHit`, `quickhack`, `explosion`, `hitZone`, `npcType`, `rarity`, `affiliation`, `isCivilian`, `isCrowd`, `isPolice`, `isCyberpsycho`, `isMaxTac`, `distance`, `lockedAtDeath`, `lethal` (from `ENeutralizeType`), and the running combat counters. Names are resolved only for the first few kills of a fight (the display name is a text lookup), the rest keep codes. The record is a plain table of numbers and short strings (under 200 bytes), so the ring of 16 records costs under 4 KB.

2. **No kill, hit, health or fact events.** The repo has only combat on/off and one looked-at hostile. Section 2 lists what to add; it is a small redscript collector plus a handful of Observes.

5. **Mode awareness.** The two modes exist only in the quest facts; reading them is trivial (section 1.3), but nothing in the repo uses `mq007_*`.

## 4. Rate and latency design notes

### 4.1 How often events fire in a typical fight

No real fight timeline was captured in this repo (the two earlier live fights recorded phases, not event counts), so the rates below are estimates from the structure of the events, to be replaced by a one-fight counter probe (section 6). Each counter is a plain integer bump in a callback; the probe costs nothing and prints at combat end.

| Event | Fires | Estimated rate in a 20 to 60 s street fight (3 to 8 hostiles) | Worth a remark? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Combat state change | once at start, once at end (the repo already waits 4 quiet seconds to call the end) | 2 per fight | Yes, both (opening is the existing 8-word warning; closing is the 20-word outcome) |
| Bullet hit / `OnHit` style damage event | per bullet or per damage tick | 2 to 15 per second at the peak (pistol 2 to 4, rifle or enemy burst fire more) | No. Use only as a counter; never one remark per hit |
| Kill (`gmpl_npc_killed_by_player` pulse or the death handler) | once per kill | one per 3 to 10 s, in bursts of 2 to 3 | Yes, filtered (headshot, streak, last enemy) |
| Non-lethal neutralisation (defeated, incapacitated, downed pulses) | once per body | similar to kills in a Pacifist run | Yes in Pacifist mode, filtered |
| Player damage taken | per hit on V | 0.3 to 3 per second under fire | Only through bands (see below) |
| V health band crossing | state change | 0 to 3 per fight with hysteresis | Yes: low health is a priority event |
| Reload | per magazine | one per 6 to 12 s of firing | Rarely: one in 3 reloads at most, only with enemies alive |
| Smart-weapon lock count change | on lock and unlock | several per second while sweeping | No remark; count only |
| Quickhack executed | per hack | 0 to 1 per 4 to 8 s for a netrunner build, none otherwise | Occasionally |
| Stealth state / detected | on state change | a few per stealth segment | Yes: being spotted (once), perfect stealth clear (once) |
| NCPD heat stage | on stage change | 0 to 2 per fight | Yes |
| Companion kills | per kill by a follower | comparable to V's | Rarely: only when V did nothing for a while |
| Civilian casualty | per civilian death near V | rare, 0 to 2 | Yes in Pacifist mode: top priority |
| Vehicle events | on mount/hit/driveby | few per chase | Occasionally |

Meaningful candidate events: about 0.1 to 0.3 per second at peak, 3 to 12 per fight. The vanilla Skippy graph speaks at most once per 20 to 45 s outside kills, and each line is 2 to 7.7 s long (mean 4.8 s). An AI companion that comments on every candidate would speak about every 4 s, far more than the game's own design, and would talk over itself because spoken remarks take 2 to 5 s.

### 4.2 Bark budget (proposal)

- **Global gap:** at most one remark started per 10 s; after a remark ends, 2.5 s of silence (the repo's shared reply owner already uses a 2 s gap). Token-bucket form: capacity 2, refill one token per 10 s, so a fight opens with a burst of at most 2 and then settles to 6 per minute. A fight of 30 s therefore holds 3 to 5 remarks, matching the 8 to 12 candidates after filtering.
- **Per-class cooldowns** (seconds): combat start 60 (once per encounter); combat end once per encounter; headshot kill 12; ordinary kill 20; streak (3 kills within 8 s) 30; reload 30 and only with enemies alive; damage taken 25; low health 20 with hysteresis (enter below 30 percent, leave above 45); stealth detected 30; heat stage up 45; quickhack 25; vehicle 30; companion kill 40; civilian casualty 15.
- **Priority tiers.** P0 may interrupt a remark that is already playing (cut within 300 ms, fade not hard cut): V below 15 percent health, a civilian killed in Pacifist mode, a boss or cyberpsycho start, V downed. P1 may displace a queued remark but never a playing one: headshot or streak, lethal kill in Pacifist mode, heat stage up. P2 never displaces anything and is dropped, not queued, if the gap is not open: ordinary kill, reload, quickhack, stealth banter.
- **Drop, do not replay.** The repo already skips a missed window rather than replaying it (`docs/BATTLE_REACTIONS.md`). A remark about a kill that is more than the maximum delay old is silence.
- **Pacifist reactions to lethal kills.** Pacifist is `aim_at_head == 0` (or `turned_pacifist >= 1`). In that mode a lethal pulse (`gmpl_npc_killed_by_player`) is a P1 disapproval, one per 12 s, escalating with a per-encounter count (first mild, third pointed, then silence, so it nags once, not forever). A non-lethal neutralisation (`defeated`, `incapacitated`, `downed`, takedown) is a P2 approval at 20 s. A civilian casualty is P0 and exempt from the per-encounter cap. In Killer mode swap the tone (kills approved, non-lethal takedowns lightly mocked) with the same budget. Attribution matters: only count a kill as "Skippy's" when the weapon in the kill event is Skippy's item; kills by other guns, melee, quickhacks, vehicles or companions get their own, rarer remark (the vanilla graph also only counts while Skippy is equipped).
- **Never speak** during scripted scenes, dialogue choices, phone calls, menus, or while the manual F6/F8/mic conversation owns the reply lock (reuse the existing gates in `mod/battle-observer.lua` `canDeliver`). Respect the existing speech owner so Skippy and a companion never talk together; if Skippy is a companion-grade speaker, a companion line has priority over P1/P2 Skippy lines.

### 4.3 What makes a remark feel timely, and the 1.5 s ceiling

A remark is timely when it lands inside the beat that caused it: the kill is still on screen, the next enemy has not died. The comfortable window is about 0.3 to 1.5 s after the event, and the hard ceiling for a reactive (kill, headshot, hit, reload) remark is 1.5 s; lines later than that are dropped. Slower events may have a longer window: combat start 2.5 s (the game itself delays a companion's start-combat bark by 1.5 s), low health 1.5 s, combat end 4 to 8 s (it is already deferred by a 4 s quiet window, and it feels natural late), heat stage 3 s.

The live turn latency is the problem. The measured chain from `docs/LATENCY_2026-10-13.md`: gateway 0.56 to 0.8 s to the model's first byte, 25 to 270 ms for the first sentence, 0.8 to 1.1 s for the first PCM from the Voxtral voice, plus about 100 to 250 ms of file, queue and status hops. (These are the measurements at `4e0e8c4b`; `main`'s copy of that document lists later items as built but not yet tried in the game: streaming the first sentence on the voice-key route, a roughly eight times faster lip-sync kernel, a kept-alive Mistral connection. They may shave a few hundred milliseconds off the Omni and voice-key routes; none changes the order of magnitude.) That is 1.6 to 2.5 s from event to sound at best for a freshly generated remark, so a freshly generated per-kill remark cannot meet 1.5 s. Options, cheapest first:

1. **Speculative pre-generation (recommended).** At combat start (the game's own start-combat delay gives about 1.5 s, and the vanilla Skippy has a 20 s grace) and again during lulls, generate a small bank of short lines per event class (for instance 2 each for: headshot, ordinary kill, streak, hit taken, low health, reload, last enemy, civilian casualty, pacifist disapproval, pacifist approval), conditioned on facts known then: mode, enemy faction names from `battle-context.mjs`, district, time of day. Render and keep the PCM in memory. On an event, pick the best unused line and start it with only the local dispatch latency (about 30 to 100 ms; the game checks `reply.json` every 30 ms while a reply is due). Refill in the background, with no more than one generation in flight and never during a manual turn. Cost: about 10 generations per fight against the free-tier interaction budget (100k per month shared with companions; see the memory note), so use a cheap or local model for the bank and keep Convai for the manual and opening/closing lines.
2. **Pre-rendered classes with slot-free text.** Lines contain no numbers or names that could be stale, so any banked line is true whenever it fires (rule from `battle-context.mjs`: never invent counts, victory or kills).
3. **Streaming first sentence.** For events that tolerate 2 s (combat start, closing), use the existing first-sentence streaming path rather than the bank.
4. **Latency accounting.** Stamp the event in Lua at the callback (`os.clock` on the game thread, one number), carry it in the request, and let the bridge stage clock report event to audible; the target is p95 under 1.5 s for banked lines and under 2.5 s for generated ones, measured, not assumed.

FPS rule (top priority in this repo): no per-frame work, no polling for combat. Everything above is an event callback that bumps a counter or sets a flag; decisions happen on a 0.25 to 0.5 s low-rate tick that exists only while combat is on, the pattern `battle-observer.lua` already uses at its half-second policy poll, or on the event itself.

## 6. Live probes needed (one batch, game open, read-only)

Run once in the game with Skippy in V's inventory (the quest item can be added with a CET console `Game.AddToInventory` or by doing the pickup), then fight. Ordered by value. Each probe only registers a callback or reads on demand; none polls per frame.

1. **Fact semantics.** Register for `gmpl_npc_killed_by_player`, `gmpl_npcs_killed_by_player`, `gmpl_npc_defeated_by_player`, `gmpl_npc_incapacitated_by_player`, `gmpl_npc_downed_by_player`, `gmpl_player_range_attack`, `gmpl_player_quickhack` with `QuestsSystem.RegisterEntity` from a small redscript, log name, value and time. Answers: does a listener fire for a same-value write (two kills inside the 1.00 s pulse), does the counter change on every kill including takedowns, does a pulse stay at 1 after a takedown (that path schedules no reset), does it fire with Skippy not equipped, how soon after the death.
2. **Equip and draw events.** Log `OnItemEquipped`-style equip callbacks and the weapon state transitions while swapping Skippy in and out; confirm `GetItemInSlot` and `GetActiveWeapon` give `Items.mq007_skippy`.
3. **Death hook payload.** On `NPCPuppet` death (redscript `@wrapMethod`), log the instigator, the weapon item id, the hit flags (headshot, critical), the hit shape, the target's `NPCType`, rarity, affiliation and a civilian or crowd flag, and the distance. Confirms what a kill record can contain at a zero polling cost.
4. **Mode facts live.** Read `mq007_skippy_aim_at_head`, `mq007_skippy_turned_pacifist`, `mq007_opt_A`, `mq007_skippy_goes_emo`, `mq007_skippy_enemies_lethal`, `..._non_lethal`, `..._total` before and after fights, in both modes (flip with `Game.SetDebugFact` in a throwaway save).
5. **Item record flags.** List the flat names of `Items.mq007_skippy`, `Items.mq007_skippy_inline0` and `Items.Preset_Yukimura_Skippy`, to confirm which smart-gun flag the fact feeds and whether the item is a SmartLink weapon.
6. **Smart-gun lock and ammo.** Read the smart-gun blackboard and the ammo blackboard once per second during a fight to learn their field names and ranges; then switch to callbacks if the fields support listeners.
7. **Stat-pool listener.** Register a health listener on V with thresholds (30, 15 percent) and confirm it fires on crossings only.
8. **One-fight counter run.** With the callbacks in 3 and 7, count every event class per fight for three fights (street, vehicle, boss or cyberpsycho) to replace the estimates in section 4.1.
9. **Silencing or sharing the vanilla barks.** Test whether setting `mq007_interrupt_skippy_dialogue` or stopping the running scene silences a vanilla bark within 100 ms, and what happens to the 20 to 45 s vanilla pauses afterwards.
10. **Prevention and district.** Read the heat stage, `PreventionSystem` events and the district name when entering a district and when heat changes.
11. **Companions.** With a main-mod companion killing an enemy, check the instigator on the death event and whether the `gmpl` facts fire (they are for V only).
12. **Damage listener semantics.** Register a `ScriptedDamageSystemListener` with `DamageSystem.RegisterListener` for V's `EntityID` with `HitTriggered` and `MissTriggered`; shoot an enemy, then let an enemy shoot V. Answers: is the registeree the instigator or the target, does `MissTriggered` fire for V's misses, how many calls per second in a firefight (replace the estimates in 4.1), and does a projected (aim-preview) hit reach the callback.
13. **Observing private methods from CET.** Try `Observe('NPCPuppet', 'SendDataTrackingEvent', ...)` and `Observe('PreventionSystem', 'OnHeatChanged', ...)`; if CET refuses them, the redscript `@wrapMethod` route is the only one.
