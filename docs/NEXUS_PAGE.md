[size=4][b]Key features[/b][/size]
[b]Talk to anyone[/b][list]
[*][b]Voice or text.[/b] Face someone and press your talk key: hold it while you speak and let go to send, or type. They answer out loud with subtitles, cinematic or above the speaker, and now and then ask you something back.
[*][b]Hold to talk, instantly.[/b] The microphone opens the moment you press the talk key, so your first words are not lost, and it stays ready for 15 seconds after a voice turn. After a pause the first press shows Opening microphone and then Speak now. Prefer to leave your hands free? Turn on Voice activation in Settings > Voices (off by default) and the mod hears when you start speaking.
[*][b]Talk with a controller.[/b] On Companion Manager > Key bindings give Talk by voice a controller button, for example the left stick click, then hold it while you look at someone, speak and let go to send.
[*][b]Nobody in front of you? Your party answers.[/b] Press the talk key without facing anyone and the first member of your party answers. The group keys take the first plus two others.
[*][b]Key bindings page.[/b] Companion Manager > Key bindings gives each action a keyboard key or a controller button from inside the game, with no restart and no trip to Cyber Engine Tweaks. Five keys are set from the start: F7 talk by voice, F6 talk by text, F10 group voice chat, F8 group text chat and F4 the Companion Manager. Change or clear any of them, and Restore defaults brings them back. A key can also be a combination such as Shift+F5, set by holding Shift, Ctrl or Alt while you press it. Johnny has his own talk keys there, with no key set at first.
[*][b]The game's named cast.[/b] 126 named characters: Panam, Johnny, Judy, Takemura, Kerry, River, Alt Cunningham and many more, each with a personality researched from the game's own lore.
[*][b]The whole city.[/b] Gangers, NCPD officers, guards, ripperdocs, vendors, bartenders and passers-by answer as their kind of person (everyone you can aim at, receptionists and quest extras included): 40 kinds of people, with 80 personalities (a woman's and a man's for each, MaxTac, NUSA and joytoys included), and shopkeepers you come back to remember you. Every nameless passer-by gets their own Night City name that fits who they are (a Valentino gets a Latino name, a Tyger Claw a Japanese one), from about 13,000 names in 16 cultures, shown on the scanner and in the subtitles. Quest crowds such as the patrons of the Afterlife answer too, with a speaking face.
[*][b]Talk has consequences.[/b] Threaten an ordinary pedestrian and they react: they walk or run off, call the police, or, if they are a ganger, merc or guard, fight back. It depends on who they are and where you are. Story and quest characters are never affected.
[*][b]Story characters where they stand.[/b] Turn on Talk to story characters (off by default) and the game's own Judy, Jackie, Panam or Rogue answer in place, with voice and face only. The story is left alone.
[*][b]Group chat.[/b] Talk to two or three companions at once. They take turns and pick up from each other.
[*][b]Phone Chat and holocalls.[/b] Text any character that has a profile here, not only the game's contacts, from a Chat tab on V's phone (with the same J invite and L dismiss buttons as the Contacts list), or call them and watch them pick up live on screen.
[*][b]No waiting.[/b] Characters you look at or walk up to get their conversation ready in the background, so they answer straight away.
[*][b]Replies that sound like people.[/b] Characters echo your question back less, lean on stock closing lines less, and no longer recite where they are or what the weather is. Their character text is leaner, with lore fixes for many named characters.
[/list][b]Companions[/b][list]
[*][b]Invite almost anyone[/b] from Characters to invite, plus hundreds more character models from the Model browser. With Mod Configuration Menu, type a name in its Search settings box to find a model at once, Rita Wheeler included, and every model shows a proper name. No party size limit.
[*][b]They live alongside you.[/b] They walk beside you, follow you through doors, lifts and fast travel, sit with you on couches, benches and bar stools, ride in your car and fight with the game's own combat AI.
[*][b]Immersive invitations (new, off by default).[/b] Switch it on in Settings > Gameplay > Immersive invitations and inviting a character becomes an appointment: they come to you on foot or by car, text when they arrive and wait a while. You can meet them somewhere on the way, or cancel it with plain words like "never mind, cancel that", also when you say it to someone else. If the car cannot be used, the character walks instead, and the game log says why. She looks for a street from the traffic cars and the ground, drives up and parks near you, and still walks when no street is found.
[*][b]Housemates (new, off by default).[/b] Switch it on in Settings > Gameplay > Housemates and a character you tell they can stay at your place moves into one of your apartments. They stay in your party until you tell them to leave, then go home and live there. They no longer vanish when the flat has a story fact on: they stay in your own apartment and step out of the way only during real scenes. In Settings > Gameplay > Housemates you can also pick the Move-in apartment (Ask me, or one of the apartments you own), set the Pause between chats (1, 2, 5 or 10 minutes), let 2 to 4 housemates join one chat with Housemates in one chat, and move everyone out with the Evict all housemates button. Residents show up right away and stay visible as you move around. Housemate chats flow from line to line with short gaps and end naturally, and the repeating "could not join" notice now shows once.
[*][b]At home in your flat.[/b] While you stay in your own apartment, companions sit down on seats by themselves instead of trailing you, and on a bar stool they face the counter.
[*][b]Revenge ambushes (new, off by default).[/b] Switch it on in Settings > Gameplay > Revenge ambushes and a gang you keep killing may, rarely, send a small squad after you, with a short threat to shout. Never in a fight, a scene, a call, a vehicle, indoors, in a safe zone or while wanted.
[*][b]Companions change outfits (new, off by default).[/b] Switch it on in Settings > Gameplay > Companions change outfits and companions change into other looks, only while you are not looking. One who stood up to change sits down again within seconds.
[*][b]They speak up.[/b] Now and then a companion starts a remark by themselves at a calm moment. Settings > Conversations > Companions speak up (Speaks up rarely by default, Off turns it off).
[*][b]Ask them to drive.[/b] Set a waypoint, have your car nearby and say something like "can you drive us to the waypoint?". They take the wheel and you get in the passenger seat.
[*][b]Point out a target.[/b] Tag an enemy with your scanner (or look right at them) and tell them to attack.
[*][b]Send them home.[/b] Tell them to leave, or use Dismiss in Current party.
[*][b]They react.[/b] A short comment when a fight starts and after it, and remarks on the loot you pick up. Both are on by default (Settings > Gameplay: React to battles and React to collected loot) and have their own cap on the shared Convai key.
[*][b]Quieter streets in a talk.[/b] The game's own ambient barks of people nearby stay muted for the whole conversation, also in the gaps between turns.
[*][b]Night City Allies followers talk.[/b] NCA followers who are characters here talk as themselves, and Lucy (with Lucy for NCA) and Rita Wheeler have their own voices and memory. NCA keeps doing the following, fighting and driving.
[/list][b]Faces and voices[/b][list]
[*][b]Faces that act.[/b] Lips follow the words, the face changes at the very word where the feeling shifts, and eyes and head follow you.
[*][b]Bodies that act.[/b] They gesture, nod and hold idle poses with props such as a cigarette, a cup or a pair of shades.
[*][b]Voices that sound like them.[/b] Pocket TTS (the default) runs on your processor with no setup, Voxtral 3 makes each voice in the cloud with a Mistral key (one steady voice per passer-by), and OmniVoice (NVIDIA) can laugh and sigh. Natural speech adds pauses and hesitations.
[*][b]V speaks too.[/b] Lines you type are voiced in the V voice that matches yours, with Pocket. Turn on Settings > Voices > V speaks your microphone lines (off by default) and the lines you speak are repeated in V's voice too, with the reply following V's line. OmniVoice for V is offered only while your companions use OmniVoice (Settings > Voices), so it never loads just for V. Use a mod that replaces V's voice? V's lines follow it by themselves, or give V your own clip in the character editor.
[*][b]Voices that hold up.[/b] The voice workers recover from a stuck start, a Pocket line that breaks up refills with more audio each time, a stalled Voxtral request is asked again in parallel, and V's lines are no longer dropped. If a voice fails after a reply has started, you still get the whole reply as text, even with Subtitles off. The voice workers also start when the Runtime sits behind a symlink or junction above its app folder.
[*][b]OmniVoice on AMD Radeon cards (experimental).[/b] Untested on AMD hardware. You install AMD's ROCm PyTorch in your own Python and the mod downloads nothing for it. On a PC with an AMD card and no NVIDIA card the game points you to the steps by itself.
[/list][b]Story and memory[/b][list]
[*][b]They know the moment.[/b] They pick up from the game's own dialogue and know your quest, your clothes and what you carry. With Convai they also see a fresh glimpse of the game window (never saved).
[*][b]Quest givers explain the job.[/b] Ask the character who gave you a job something like "what do I do next?" and they walk you through the step you are on, with the places, people and a tip, never beyond where you are. It covers 171 main jobs, side jobs and gigs, Phantom Liberty included.
[*][b]They know who you are to them.[/b] Strangers no longer act as if they knew V, "I gave you eddies" is understood as a payment from V to them, and a companion knows which weapon they carry, also when it is holstered.
[*][b]They remember you.[/b] Conversations stay with a character across saves, and Judy, Panam, River, Kerry and Meredith know where things stand with V. Make one character, or everyone, forget at any time.
[*][b]Johnny in your head.[/b] Turn it on (off by default), give a key to Talk to Johnny by voice or by text, and Johnny answers in V's head wherever you look. He knows your jobs, his own history and how things stand between you. Choose Speaks up rarely or Speaks up often and he also comments on people you meet, jobs you finish and romances. He is only busy during real scenes, such as a cutscene with a choice on screen or a call, and should no longer turn you away while you ride a lift or stand around.
[/list][b]Make it yours[/b][list]
[*][b]Character editor.[/b] Edit any character's bio, speaking style and memories in your browser, or make characters of your own for anyone who has no profile (Mistral or Local AI). Open it from the Character editor button on Home in the Companion Manager.
[*][b]World rules.[/b] Write rules for the whole of Night City on the editor's World rules page, for example "nobody knows V's name unless V says it", and every character follows them from the next reply, on every provider.
[*][b]Prompting.[/b] On the editor's Prompting tab, tune the prompt for each AI option: Mistral, Local AI standard and lite, the Mature models and every Custom model you add. Edit the output rules, example replies, opening and closing lines, sampling (temperature, Top P, Top K, Min P and the repeat, presence and frequency penalties) and length limits, add any other setting your model server understands in the Advanced parameters box (JSON, Local AI and Custom), watch a live preview and reset any part. Where Mature is on, the Mature wording is yours to edit too. The game facts, the safety rules and the Mature hard limits stay locked, and Convai's prompting lives on Convai's side.
[*][b]Content packs.[/b] A content pack is a small add-on made only of a settings file and some text, with no code of its own. It changes how the characters talk: world rules, prompt wording, a character's backstory, style and name, name pools for passers-by, Convai twins and a separate memory. Switch each pack on or off in Settings > Content packs. Your own edits in the editor still win over a pack. [url=[MEDIEVAL_URL]][b]Medieval AI NPCs[/b][/url], a separate add-on page, is the first one and turns Night City into a medieval world.
[*][b]Tuned for each model.[/b] Mistral, Local AI standard and lite and the Mature models each start from a prompt tuned for them and tested on sample conversations, so they slip into fewer echoes, lectures and off-character lines.
[*][b]Choose what they know.[/b] Settings > Conversations > Dynamic info has twelve switches: Location, Time and weather, Company and vehicle, Health, heat and fights, V's money, V's gear and clothes, V's gender and lifepath, Quest and journal, Recent game dialogue, Time since last talk, Music and TV (new: the radio station and song playing, and the TV channel) and Other mods' state (off by default, it reads Dark Future and the Equipment-EX outfit V wears). Turn one off and it is left out of what the characters are told, on every provider.
[*][b]Voice clips.[/b] Give a character a new voice from a 5 to 20 second recording. It applies without a restart.
[*][b]Bring your own AI.[/b] The Custom provider works with OpenRouter, Nano-GPT, Ollama, LM Studio or any OpenAI-compatible service, and Local AI can run your own model files (see below). Switch provider in Settings and the very next line uses it.
[*][b]Any language.[/b] Menus in sixteen languages (all fifteen translations were reviewed and fixed, with the game's official names for Johnny), characters that answer in English, or in the language you write in when Settings > Voices > Voice language is on Automatic (a short line never switches it), or in a fixed language, and microphone packs for European and Asian languages. Spoken other languages need the OmniVoice, Voxtral or Convai voice. Pocket speaks English, and French, Italian, German, Spanish and Portuguese with an optional PocketTTS model. With a [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/tree/main/multilingual]multilingual pack[/url], OmniVoice and Voxtral speak in the game's own dubbed voices for ten languages.
[/list][b]Light on your game[/b][list]
[*]The heavy work runs in a separate background service, not inside the game, and the default setup uses no video memory.
[*]Unused models unload on their own, and Local AI steps down to a lighter model when the game needs the memory. On cards from 12 to 24 GB it leaves more room for the game.
[*]Works with Vortex, Mod Organizer 2 or a manual install.
[*]A stale player-memory lock no longer stops the background service from starting, and the logs are quieter. Less repeated background work while you play (loot, music and TV, party and housemate checks). The log now has start-up timing lines, and error reports are clearer.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Recommended settings[/b][/size]
Pick one in Companion Manager > Settings > Conversation provider.
[b]1. Cloud: the best option[/b] (fastest replies, light on your PC, no extra downloads)[list]
[*][b]Convai (best):[/b] already set up, nothing to do. It is the most complete: characters act on what you ask and see where you are. The free tier gives 500 turns a day and Settings shows how many are left. A dropped connection is retried once by itself before the reply starts.
[*][b]Mistral (second choice):[/b] for more free turns, make a free [url=https://console.mistral.ai]Mistral[/url] account (phone verification), create an API key, choose [b]Mistral[/b] and paste the key once: about 5,000 turns a day.
[*][b]Voice:[/b] Pocket TTS (the default) runs on your processor with no limit. With a Mistral key, choose [b]Voxtral[/b] in Settings > Voices > Companion voices for voices closest to the characters. If Voxtral cannot speak (no key, a limit, a Mistral plan that does not include it), replies show as text until it can: the mod never switches your voice to another one.
[/list][b]2. Local: no internet, no account, no limit[/b][list]
[*]Needs a graphics card with room to spare next to the game: 12 GB of video memory or more (see the card list below). On an 8 GB card the model may not fit whole: Local AI then runs part of it on your processor, which works but is slower, and tells you once. Cloud text is faster there.
[*]Install a Local AI pack: [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/blob/main/local-ai/LocalAI-Standard-Gemma-4-E4B.zip][b]Local AI standard[/b][/url], or [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/blob/main/local-ai/LocalAI-Lite-Bonsai-2-4B.zip][b]Local AI lite[/b][/url] for smaller graphics cards. A Mature pack works too.
[*]Choose [b]Local AI[/b]. With only a Mature pack, also turn on [b]Mature content[/b].
[*]Already run models in KoboldCpp, LM Studio or Ollama, or have your own GGUF file? The mod can use them: see [b]Use your own AI model or model server[/b] below.
[*]Voice: [b]Pocket[/b] (already in the Runtime, English), or [b]OmniVoice[/b] with [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/blob/main/voice/OmniPack-OmniVoice-0.6B.zip]its pack[/url] on an NVIDIA card with 12 GB of video memory or more, for voices that sound like the game characters and for other languages.
[*]It runs on your graphics card (Vulkan, NVIDIA or AMD), needs at least 16 GB of system memory and shares the card with the game, so it is slower than the cloud on most PCs.
[*]Local stays local: if your local voice or Local AI fails, you get a message or text only. The mod never switches to a cloud voice or cloud text on its own.
[*]Fully offline: if Windows asks whether Node.js may use the network, you can decline, and turn off [b]Settings > Privacy > Send error reports automatically[/b].
[/list]

[b]How much video memory?[/b]
The game itself uses most of the card: roughly 6 to 7 GB at 1080p, 9 to 10 GB at 1440p or with ray tracing, and 12 GB or more at 4K or with path tracing. Local AI and OmniVoice need room on top of that:[list]
[*][b]8 GB card:[/b] cloud text (Convai or Mistral) with Pocket or Voxtral is the fastest. Local AI still starts: whatever does not fit next to the game runs on your processor, so replies are slower, and the mod says so once. OmniVoice is not started on a card this small: the mod uses Pocket straight away and tells you once.
[*][b]10 to 12 GB:[/b] Local AI lite with Pocket at 1080p without ray tracing, or OmniVoice with cloud text.
[*][b]12 to 16 GB:[/b] Local AI standard with Pocket at 1080p, or lite with ray tracing or 1440p.
[*][b]16 GB or more:[/b] Local AI standard with ray tracing or 1440p and up, or Local AI with OmniVoice.
[/list]What each voice + text pair adds on top of the game:[list]
[*]Pocket or Voxtral voice + Convai or Mistral: [b]0 GB[/b] (the default)
[*]Pocket voice + Local AI lite: about 1.8 GB
[*]Pocket voice + Local AI standard: about 3.1 GB (needs about 6 GB free to start)
[*]OmniVoice + Convai or Mistral: about 2 GB (up to 4 GB)
[*]OmniVoice + Local AI lite: about 3.9 GB (up to 5.9 GB)
[*]OmniVoice + Local AI standard: about 5.2 GB (up to 7.3 GB)
[/list]Measured on a 12 GB RTX 4080 Laptop. If your card runs short, the mod steps down by itself (standard to lite, then part of the model on your processor, never to the cloud), and you can change either choice in Settings at any time. Lowering texture quality in the game frees the most room.

[b]Steps[/b]
[list=1]
[*]Install the mods in Requirements.
[*]Download [b]Scripts[/b] from this page (the main file for game 2.5, or the optional file for the older 2.31, Vortex or by hand) and the Runtime from Hugging Face: [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/blob/main/Runtime.zip][b]Runtime for game 2.5[/b][/url] or [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/blob/main/game-2.31/Runtime.zip][b]Runtime for game 2.31[/b][/url]. Use the same version and the same game for both.
[*]Close the game. Extract both zips into your [b]Cyberpunk 2077 folder[/b] (the one with bin, archive and r6) and let the folders merge. If Windows asks about files with the same names, choose Replace: it is always safe. Don't extract into a new folder named after the zip: Windows' "Extract All" and 7-Zip's "Extract to" suggest one. Extract the Runtime before the OmniVoice pack.
[*]Start the game. The first start takes a little longer while the voice service prepares itself.
[*]Check the keys of the AI NPC Cyberpunk Mod actions. F7 talk by voice, F6 talk by text, F10 group voice chat, F8 group text chat and F4 the Companion Manager are set from the start. Change them, or give a key to Talk to Johnny, in game under [b]Companion Manager > Key bindings[/b] or in Cyber Engine Tweaks' [b]Bindings[/b]. A key you set before stays as it was.
[/list]

[b]Vortex[/b][list]
[*]Add the Scripts with Vortex's download button on this page, or drop the Scripts zip onto Vortex's Mods page. Drop the Runtime zip from Hugging Face onto the Mods page too, then click Deploy. Every file lands in the right place. Vortex moves only the HOW TO INSTALL text file to its mod-extra-files folder, which is harmless.
[*]The warning sign in Vortex's Version column only means the mod was added from a file and not downloaded from Nexus. It is harmless.
[*]Local AI and Mature packs: Vortex shows "You Have Reached The Fallback Installer". Choose "Yes, Install To Staging Anyway" and every file lands correctly. With two of these packs Vortex reports "unresolved file conflicts" because they share identical files (the licences and the embedding model). Pick either one to load after the other, it makes no difference.
[*]The OmniVoice pack (Voice models): extract it into the game folder by hand. Vortex can't install it, because it holds DLLs.
[*]Updating: drop the new Scripts zip, then the Runtime update zips one by one, oldest first, onto the Mods page. When Vortex asks, choose "Replace the existing mod" (it may first ask to replace the file, say yes), then click Deploy. The full Runtime and the switch zip (for moving from game 2.31 to 2.5) work the same way.
[*]If Vortex shows External Changes ("Mod files were changed outside Vortex") for this mod's files after an update, click Save all changes (or Use newer file for all changes) so it keeps the newer files, never Revert, then Confirm.
[/list][b]Mod Organizer 2[/b]
Install the Runtime as one mod. Put the Runtime update zips, the multilingual packs and the PocketTTS models INTO that Runtime mod's folder (replace files), not into mods of their own. The other packs can be mods of their own.

[b]Keep the Scripts and the Runtime together.[/b] Both must be for your game version (2.5 or 2.31) and both on the same mod version. Settings > Help shows the version of each. Mixing them gives odd errors.

[b]Updating[/b]
Updating? Download the new Scripts (the main file for game 2.5, or the optional file for 2.31), and instead of the full Runtime get the small [b]Runtime update[/b] zips from the updates folder on Hugging Face ([url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/tree/main/updates]game 2.5[/url] or [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/tree/main/game-2.31/updates]game 2.31[/url]) and extract them over your install one by one, starting from your version. The two games have separate update folders, so never mix them. Choose Replace if Windows asks. Your settings and memories are kept. Never delete the mod's folders to update: the optional packs live inside them and would be gone. The optional packs (OmniVoice, Local AI, Mature) have not changed, so if you already have them you don't need to download them again. If you skip one, the setup check in Settings > Help names the update zip that is missing. Not sure which version you have? Use the full Runtime. Moved your game from 2.31 to 2.5? A Runtime from the release before game 2.5 can take the first zip in the [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/tree/main/updates]game 2.5 updates folder[/url], which switches it to 2.5, and then the next zips one by one. Any other Runtime for 2.31 needs the full Runtime for game 2.5.

[b]Optional packs[/b]
All on the same [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/tree/main]Hugging Face page[/url]. Each is one zip that works on its own. Install none, some or all, the same way as the Runtime.[list]

[size=4][b]Use your own AI model or model server[/b][/size]
Already run AI models on your PC? Local AI can use them. Create a text file named [b]manifest.custom.json[/b] (check it does not end in .txt) in %LOCALAPPDATA%\AiNpcCyberpunkMod\local-ai\ (paste that into the Explorer address bar and make the local-ai folder if it is not there). It is outside the game folder, so it survives reinstalls and mod managers. The older place, Cyberpunk 2077\red4ext\plugins\AiNpcCyberpunkMod\app\local-ai\, still works and both are read, but a clean reinstall deletes it: to keep a setup you made there, copy its custom folder and manifest.custom.json into the %LOCALAPPDATA% folder.

[b]Your own model server[/b] (KoboldCpp, LM Studio, Ollama, llama.cpp, or anything with an OpenAI-compatible API). Start the server first, then:
[code]{"version":1,"models":[{"endpoint":"http://127.0.0.1:5001/v1","name":"My Server"}]}[/code]
Usual addresses: KoboldCpp http://127.0.0.1:5001/v1, LM Studio http://127.0.0.1:1234/v1 (start its local server), Ollama http://127.0.0.1:11434/v1 (also add "model":"qwen3:8b", or whichever model you pulled). If your server wants a password or key, add "apiKey":"your-key". No Local AI pack is needed.

[b]Your own model file[/b] (any GGUF). Put it in a folder named custom next to the manifest, then:
[code]{"version":1,"models":[{"file":"custom/MyModel-Q4_K_M.gguf","name":"My Model"}]}[/code]
This runs on the mod's own engine, so a Local AI or Mature pack must be installed.

Then in game: Settings > Conversation provider, choose Local AI, pick your model in the Local AI model row. List several models in the same file, separated by commas. Your model shares the graphics card with the game, so pick a size that leaves the game enough video memory.

[size=4][b]Help and FAQ[/b][/size]
[b]Is the 2.31 build the same mod?[/b]
Yes, the same mod from the same source, built once for each game version. Game 2.5 is the normal build, and the 2.31 build is kept for players who stay on the older game.

[b]A new game hangs on game 2.5.[/b]
That is Cyber Engine Tweaks 1.38.0, not this mod. Start the new game without Cyber Engine Tweaks installed, save once, then install it again.

[b]Will it slow my game down?[/b]
The heavy work runs in a separate background service, not inside the game, and the default setup uses no video memory. See How much video memory? above for the other options.

[b]Does it change the story?[/b]
No. Companions are temporary copies that the mod owns. The game's own story characters are left alone unless you turn on Settings > Conversations > Talk to story characters (then reload your save). They then answer with voice and face only.

[b]What leaves my computer?[/b]
With Convai, your lines go to Convai, along with a recent picture of only the game window so characters can see where you are. It is taken only while someone you can talk to is near, kept in memory for a few seconds and never saved. With Mistral, your lines go to Mistral. With Local AI, nothing leaves your computer for replies. The Runtime carries no Convai key of its own: it signs in for you with short-lived tokens, and if that sign-in fails you get one notice. The Runtime's security was also tightened: the hidden browser runs in a sandbox, sign-in and report handling are stricter, and more personal details are scrubbed from reports.

[b]Can I make my own characters?[/b]
Yes, with Mistral or Local AI. Face a character that has no profile here (for example a Night City Allies follower from a character mod), open Settings > Edit characters > Open and choose New character in the page that opens. Give them a name, a bio and a way of speaking. The page can fill in how the game recognises them from the character you are facing. An optional voice clip gives them a voice of their own. With Convai they talk as an ordinary person, and the game says so once.

[b]Can I change a character's voice?[/b]
Yes. In the same editor page, open a story character's Voice tab (or one of your own characters) and choose Add voice clip: a WAV, MP3, OGG or FLAC file of 5 to 20 seconds of one person speaking clearly, with the words said in it typed out. Pocket, OmniVoice and Voxtral copy that voice from that character's next line, with no restart. Remove clip brings the original voice back. The clip stays on your PC.

[b]What is a content pack?[/b]
A small add-on made only of a settings file and some text, with no code. It changes how the characters talk: world rules, prompt wording, a character's backstory, style and name, name pools for passers-by, their Convai twins and a separate memory. Install it like any add-on, by extracting its download into your Cyberpunk 2077 folder, then switch it on in Settings > Content packs. New names show after the game restarts and everything else from the next reply. Your own edits in the character editor win over a pack. [url=[MEDIEVAL_URL]]Medieval AI NPCs[/url] is the first.

[b]Error reports[/b]
When something in the mod fails, or replies stay slow, a short report goes to the developer so it can be fixed without asking you for logs. It is on by default. Turn it off in [b]Settings > Privacy > Send error reports automatically[/b].[list]
[*][b]Sent:[/b] the error, the mod and requirement versions, your provider, voice and Local AI model choices, Mature content on or off, your graphics card, processor, Windows version, security products, the game's drive, screen size, the names of your installed mods, the setup check, and up to 150 log lines around the error, with keys, IDs and your Windows user name removed. For slow replies: only how long each stage of a few recent replies took, and their token counts.
[*][b]Never sent:[/b] keys, what you or the characters say, memories, audio, your Windows user name or your IP address.
[*]Reports are kept for 30 days.
[/list]

[b]What languages can I use?[/b]
Menus come in sixteen languages. Characters answer in English by default. Settings > Voices > Voice language can be set to Automatic (the language you write in, and a short line like "hows biz" never switches it) or to a fixed language. Pocket speaks English, and French, Italian, German, Spanish and Portuguese when the optional PocketTTS model and the multilingual pack of that language are installed. Voxtral speaks the 22 languages Mistral lists for it. A reply in a language your voice does not speak is shown as text, with a one-time notice. OmniVoice speaks every language, and so does the Convai voice (with Convai as the provider), if you chose it. For Chinese, Japanese, Korean, French, German, Italian, Spanish, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese and Russian, the optional [url=https://huggingface.co/alystria-ai/ai-npc-cyberpunk-mod-packs/tree/main/multilingual]multilingual packs[/url] make OmniVoice and Voxtral sound like the game's dub in that language instead of the English actors. The Convai microphone expects English, so type in other languages. With Mistral the microphone understands other languages too. With Local AI or a Custom endpoint, the optional Microphone packs add ten European languages, Mandarin Chinese, Japanese and Korean.

[b]The Companion Manager doesn't show up.[/b]
Press your manager key (stand up first if V is sitting), or press Esc, then Mods, and choose Companion Manager (in Native Settings UI, the AI NPC Cyberpunk Mod tab). No Mods entry in the pause menu? Install or update Mod Configuration Menu (0.10.5 or newer) or Native Settings UI.

[b]Settings says it needs a browser, or the service does not start.[/b]
Install or repair Microsoft Edge (free from [url=https://www.microsoft.com/edge]microsoft.com/edge[/url]) or install Google Chrome, then restart the game. Some Windows tweak tools remove Edge. Browser installed somewhere unusual? Make a file named [b]browser.txt[/b] next to launcher.ini (red4ext\plugins\AiNpcCyberpunkMod) with the path to its exe on one line.

[b]Settings says "No model installed" for Local AI.[/b]
The pack was probably extracted into an extra folder named after the zip. Extract it again into the Cyberpunk 2077 folder itself (the mod also moves misplaced packs into place at the next start). With only a Mature pack, turn on Mature content in Settings > Conversation provider.

[b]A voice fails and the notice mentions Windows memory or the paging file.[/b]
Windows ran out of memory it could promise the voice (error 1455, the paging file is too small). The mod says so once, and Pocket takes over or the reply shows as text. Enlarge the paging file or close other programs, then restart the game.

[b]The mod does not load, or the manager will not open.[/b]
Post what you see (a screenshot helps) on this page's Posts tab or in the Discord server below.

[b]Something else?[/b]
In game, open Companion Manager > Settings > Help > Help and support, choose [b]Generate report[/b], and send that file with what you were doing.
