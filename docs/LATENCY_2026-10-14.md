## 1. The microphone route

**What is already there.** A voice-key turn on a text route (Mistral, Groq, Local AI) streams in both directions:

- The audio goes to the local speech recogniser while the player is still talking (a 100 ms chunk ten times a second; the worker decodes
  the newest 8 s about every 1.2 s, `docs/LOCAL_STT.md`). `finish()` therefore usually has the text already: 2 to 4 ms for 4 of 6 clips
  measured with real-time pacing, 0.33 to 0.45 s for the others (the last block had not been decoded yet).
- The reply no longer waits to be written whole: `startVoiceKeyStream` hands the text host a `ReplyStream`, the voice starts on the first
  complete sentence, and `onReply` merges the finished reply in (`docs/LATENCY_2026-10-13.md` 4.1). Saves 0.17 to 0.45 s on Mistral and Groq,
  about 1.2 s on the local AI.

Re-run here (`node scripts/Measure-TurnLatency.mjs --voice-key --runs 2`, real `MicrophoneSession`, local speech channel, text host and
`ReplyStream`, simulated model): the voice is handed its text at 1,511 ms (whole reply) against 666 ms (streamed) on Mistral + Voxtral,
1,002 against 741 on Groq + Voxtral, 3,012 against 1,167 on local + Voxtral. The rendering half of the harness did not start in this
worktree (it has no `vendor/` or `runtime/`, so the headless renderer is missing, `ok: false`); the rendered numbers are the ones in
`docs/LATENCY_2026-10-13.md` 4.1.

**What is left, and why it stops here: a design decision.** Two more things could start the turn sooner, and neither is free:

| Option | What it does | Saves | Costs and risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Decode more often while "Listening" | The recogniser decodes the newest 8 s every 600 ms instead of 1,200 ms, so the last block is nearly always done when the key is pressed again | the 0.33 to 0.45 s tail on the 2 of 6 clips that have one (about 0.1 s on average over all turns) | The worker's CPU roughly doubles while the key is held (now up to about 15% of a core, so about 30%): the game's FPS is the top priority, and nothing here can be judged without the game running. It is in the Python process, never the game thread, but cores are shared. |
| B. Start the model on a stable partial | When the last partial transcript has not changed for about 300 ms, send the request; if the final text differs, cancel it and send again | up to 0.3 to 0.45 s on the turns where the tail exists (the whole STT tail) and nothing on the others | A wrong partial costs a wasted request. On Groq's free plan that is a wasted share of 8,000 tokens a minute, which section 3 is trying to save; on Mistral it is only quota. The recogniser is poor on names and acted speech (`docs/LOCAL_STT.md`: 28% on game speech), so a partial is wrong often exactly where the persona matters. Needs a cancel path through the text host and the memory store. |
| C. Leave it | The tail stays. | | 0.33 to 0.45 s on about a third of the voice-key turns |

Recommendation: C for now, and A only if a live FPS check with the game running shows the extra decode is free. B is the largest gain
and the one that is most likely to misfire; it should wait for the Groq budget question and for a measured partial-stability rate.
Nothing was built for this item, because choosing between them is yours.

## 2. Pre-encoded Voxtral references

**State.** The reference is the clip cut to about 8.5 s at a pause, mono, 24 kHz, as Ogg Vorbis quality 2 (38 KB for Judy; Ogg because an
MP3 encoder low-passes at 10.5 to 11.3 kHz and the clone sounded dull; `REFERENCE_PREPARATION` version 4, `bridge/voxtral-reference.mjs`).
`bridge/voxtral-reference.mjs` keeps the result in `<references>/prepared/<voice>.ogg` with a `.json` naming the source clip's hash and the
settings. The release builder (`voxtralCache` in `packaging/build-release.mjs`) makes those slots with `VoxtralVoice.build`, the same call a
conversation makes, from the same references, and ships them in `voices/prepared/`: the Runtime package carries the Pocket clips' slots, the
OmniVoice pack the slots for the clips it adds. `prepare-state.mjs` sets `CYBERPUNK_CONVAI_OMNI_REFERENCES` to `voices/`, which is where
the bridge looks first. Encoding is bit-exact, so a slot is the same bytes in the package and on a player's machine, and the build fails
(rather than ships a WAV) when ffmpeg or libvorbis is missing. A voice with no shipped slot, or a clip that changed, is prepared when it
is warmed: with ffmpeg as Ogg (then MP3), without ffmpeg as the 24 kHz WAV.

Checked here:

- The developer's own runtime folder holds 11 Ogg and 10 MP3 slots; `alex.ogg` is 32,648 bytes. The old WAV reference of the same
  clip is about 408 KB (557 KB for the omni-long clip, 744 KB as base64), so a player without ffmpeg sends about 11 to 15 times fewer
  bytes with the shipped slot.
- New test (`bridge/voxtral-reference.test.mjs`, last test): a preparer that has no ffmpeg and an encoder that throws reads the Ogg
  slot as `cached: true, format: 'ogg'` without ever looking for ffmpeg, and a voice with no slot falls back to the WAV.
- The slot is read before ffmpeg is probed, and the cache signature does not depend on ffmpeg being present
  (`ladder()` follows `compress`, not the machine), which is why the builder's slots match on a player's machine.

**Fixed:** `docs/PACKAGING.md` ("Pre-built Mistral voice references") still said 40 kbit/s MP3 and 43 KB; it now says Ogg Vorbis q2 and
38 KB. Nothing in the code changed for this item.

**Expected saving** (from `docs/LATENCY_2026-10-12.md` section 4 and `docs/LATENCY_2026-10-13.md` 8.2: first audio 1.37 s with the 857 KB
body against 0.57 s with the 89 KB one; 64 to 32 kbit/s another 0.4 to 0.5 s): the bytes saved are the reference upload, about 0.5 to
0.8 s of first audio on this uplink for a player who has no ffmpeg. Not remeasured live: no call to Voxtral was made.

## 3. A smaller prompt for Groq's free plan

**The problem.** Groq's free plan allows 8,000 tokens a minute per model. Judy's prompt in the repo's realistic fixture is 28,750
characters (about 6,850 tokens), so a second Groq turn inside a minute was refused: with a Mistral key the turn went there, without
one it fell to Convai. And to fit even one request the budget (`bridge/prompt-budget.mjs`) was already cutting what the character
remembers, the older exchanges, the older-talk summary and the volatile notes (the Natural speech offer, clipped in the middle) on
every Groq turn.

**What was built** (all on the Groq route only; `buildMessages` is untouched, so Mistral's and the local AI's prompt is byte for
byte what it was, held by hashes taken from the commit before in `bridge/groq-prompt.test.mjs`):

1. `compactBackstory`, `compactPersona`, `compactForGroq` (`bridge/groq-prompt.mjs`): the shipped profile's `Scope:` lines are removed
   (each topic item is followed by a line that says what it does not establish; they were 20% of a named character's backstory, and the
   facts they qualify stay), the topic-material section keeps its first paragraphs up to about 2,000 characters, the example lines are
   cut to six (the repeat guard still sees all 16: it reads the host's persona, not this copy), history to the last four exchanges, and
   the line, summary and memory limits are a little tighter. Never touched: the persona's identity, style, relationships, knowledge
   limits and appearance, the output rules, the format notes, the right-now block, the world facts (the budget trims those, by its own
   priority) and the player's words. Deterministic per character, so the early part of the prompt is the same every turn.
2. `budgetedChat` takes two optional things (`bridge/prompt-budget.mjs`): `shape(args, options)`, the arguments to build from, and
   `budget().soft`, a smaller target tried first. A prompt that cannot reach the soft target is fitted to the hard limit exactly as
   before, so the target can never make a request fail. Without either, `budgetedChat` is unchanged (the Local AI route).
3. The Groq route (`groqRoute`, `bridge/text-provider-host.mjs`) uses both. The soft target is `GROQ_SOFT_TOKENS = 4900` in the
   budget's own estimate (3.6 characters a token; the real count is about 4.2, so about 3,800 real tokens a prompt, two turns a minute
   with about 100 tokens of reply). **It is used only when the player has no Mistral key**: with one, the second turn goes to Mistral with its
   whole context, so nothing optional is cut for a target. The shaping (the first bullet) applies in both cases.
4. Switch off: `CYBERPUNK_CONVAI_GROQ_COMPACT=0` (or `off`, `false`, `no`) turns the shaping and the soft target off; the route then
   behaves as on 2026-10-12.

**Measured** (`node scripts/Measure-GroqPrompt.mjs`: the real `groqRoute` chat wrapper and budget, a stub for the model call that counts
the request; 12 turns 20 s apart against a simulated 8,000-token sliding minute; tokens at 4.2 characters a token plus 6 per message):

| Prompt | Before | After, Groq key only | After, with a Mistral key | Turns Groq answers in 4 minutes (3 a minute offered) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Judy, every optional block on (Expressive emotion notes, Natural speech offer) | 6,486 tokens | 5,912 (-9%) | 5,912 | 4 of 12 before and after: one a minute |
| Judy, those blocks off | 5,728 | 3,720 (-35%) | 4,744 (-17%) | 4 before, **8 after** (two a minute) |
| Unnamed archetype NPC | 2,639 | 2,597 | 2,597 | 8 before and after |

Across all 78 named characters of the roster (`--roster`, the same fixture context): with the format notes and the volatile notes (Natural
speech, follow-up policy) emptied, the median prompt goes from 4,513 to 3,606 real tokens, and 1 of 78 prompts was under 3,900 tokens
before against **78 of 78** after (two turns a minute, Groq key only; 9 of 78 with a Mistral key, where nothing optional is cut for the
target). With both blocks on, the median goes from 5,780 to 5,450 and 0 of 78 were under 3,900 before against 9 after: those two blocks are
4,900 characters (about 1,200 tokens) the budget cannot cut, and with the output rules they leave a named character near 5,000 tokens even
with every optional block gone.

What the "Judy, everything on" row also shows: before, every Groq turn cut the remembered facts, the older exchanges, the summary and
clipped the Natural speech block; after, Judy's whole prompt fits the plan's hard limit and none of that is cut.

Latency: Groq's prefill is about 415 ms for 6,000 tokens (0.07 ms a token, `docs/LATENCY_2026-10-12.md` section 5), so the saving per turn is
about 40 ms (everything on) to 140 ms (blocks off, Groq key only). Where it counts is the turn that used to be refused: a refused request
costs the failed call and then the Mistral route (about 0.5 to 1 s more) or, with no Mistral key, Convai. Build and fit cost on the bridge
is 0.1 to 4 ms a turn (the soft attempt is a second fit), nothing per frame, nothing in the game.

**Not done, and why it needs your decision.** A named character with the Natural speech offer and the Expressive emotion notes on cannot
reach two turns a minute by cutting optional context, because those two blocks and the output rules are fixed text. Options:

| Option | Saves (estimate) | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| A. Leave it. Judy-sized characters stay at one Groq turn a minute (then Mistral, or Convai without a key). | 0 | none |
| B. A shorter wording of the output rules, the emotion notes and the Natural speech offer for Groq only | about 1,000 tokens (rules 3,100 characters to about 1,500, notes 2,200 to about 1,200, the offer 2,700 to about 1,300) | These are the texts that keep a small model in character and out of stage directions (`docs/MISTRAL_PROMPT_PASS.md`); a rewrite needs the live evaluation (`scripts/Eval-MistralPrompt.mjs`, real Groq calls), which was not allowed here. |
| C. A distilled Groq bio per named character (about 1,500 characters instead of about 5,000 after the shaping) | about 800 to 1,000 tokens | 78 bios to write and judge; changes who the character is on Groq only |

B and C together would put Judy near 3,900. Nothing for B or C was built.

**Quality caveat.** The shaping removes guard lines and the tail of the topic list from Groq's copy of the persona, and with no Mistral key
the soft target cuts memories, older history and the summary the way the hard limit already did. None of this could be judged by ear or by
a Groq call here; run `Eval-MistralPrompt` against Groq before release and, if a character drifts, set
`CYBERPUNK_CONVAI_GROQ_COMPACT=0`.
