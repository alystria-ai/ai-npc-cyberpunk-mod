# On-screen notifications

Every popup the mod shows is the game's neutral warning line (`PlayerPuppet:ConvaiPhoneFeedback`). There is one gate in front of it,
`mod/notices.lua`, and every notice belongs to an entry in its catalog. This page lists the catalog; `bridge/notices.test.mjs` keeps the two
in step and fails when a notice is added without a class.

The rule: a player should see as few notices as possible, only where they are waiting on something and would otherwise wonder, or where
something really failed and they have to act. Everything routine is for developers.

| Class | Shown | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Player** | always | An intuitive wait (a conversation starting, the microphone connecting), its outcome, or a real failure the player must act on. Short wording. An identical notice is not repeated inside its window (the "Window" column) while Developer mode is off. |
| **Developer** | Developer mode only | Routine status: dismissals, driving steps, acknowledgements, tactic outcomes, diagnostics. |
| **Removed** | never | Noise even for developers (it repeats something already on screen). |

Settings > Development > Developer mode is off by default and read with a cheap boolean when a notice is about to be shown; nothing here runs
per frame. With Developer mode on every occurrence shows (no repeat windows), so a developer sees the routine notices too.

Not notifications, and not gated: the in-menu status lines (Settings status line, Party page feedback, the phone row's own "INVITE QUEUED -
CLOSE PHONE"), the character's spoken reply and subtitles (`ConvaiShowReply`), and the chat input dialog.

## What a player sees with Developer mode off

Invitation: "{name} joined your group." when the companion arrives (every time, quick or slow), and "{name} could not prepare/join: {reason}" on a failure.
A companion's Convai connection is prepared before she joins: the invitation is ready, and so "joined" is posted, only once her connection is open (on a player's own free key, which holds one connection at a time, her connection is opened for the join and the others open when V talks to them). That is why "Preparing connection to {name}…" is rare for a party member: it appears only when V starts a conversation with someone not connected, never from looking.
Companion invitations (manager, phone and party) show three kinds of line, the same to a player and a developer: what the companion is still waiting for, "{name} joined your group." once she has joined, and failures. The wait is a line of the held loading notice (see Loading notices), not an ordinary notice: "Preparing {name}: waiting for <the step that blocks her>…", for example "Preparing Evelyn: waiting for the voice model to load…". It appears when the wait has lasted 1.5 s (a quick preparation shows nothing), names the current step, changes in place as the step changes, lists every companion that is waiting (one line each; two copies of one character share a line; from the fourth on they are counted: "Preparing 2 more companions…") and stays up until she has spawned. If she fails, the wait goes and the failure is its own notice, saying what failed and what to do. The steps: the conversation service has not answered yet (still starting, or busy loading its models), the memory service (also her turn when more than eight are preparing), the voice model loading, her conversation connection opening, and the game placing her. The party list shows each copy's stage too, and the mod log keeps every stage.
Talking: "Preparing connection to {name}…" (only when V starts a conversation, by the talk key, the composer or voice, with a character, a party copy included, who is not connected yet, whether or not a warm-up already started; never from gaze or a warm-up),
"Connecting microphone - wait for Listening", "Listening - press {key} again to finish". Failures: the conversation service starting, missing
its key, stopped or failed; no reply; the microphone unavailable or timed out; a companion action that was refused; a voice engine that fell
back (once; an OmniVoice that is only being loaded again says "not ready yet" and its return is silent). Hints for a key press that did nothing: no one to talk to, group chat needs two characters, finish the recording with the other key.

## How much a notice can say

Every notice is the game's warning line (`WarningMessageGameController`), and its frame is fixed by the game: the text is drawn in Rajdhani
Semi-Bold at 35 pt, in UPPER CASE, wraps at 800 px (the text box's `wrappingAtPosition`) and sits in a frame 851 px wide whose height follows
the text (`warning_message.inkwidget`: every plate and bracket piece fills its parent, nothing clips). The frame was drawn for one line (80 px
tall) and holds two (about 107 px: 2 x 33 px of line plus 40 px of margin); a third line makes it a tall block, six lines about 240 px.
Measured with the game's own font (the Semi-Bold glyph advances, decompressed from `raj-semibold.fnt`): an upper-case capital averages 18.7 px,
ordinary text about 15.5 px a letter with spaces, so one line holds 47 to 51 letters and two lines about 95 at best. Keep a notice for a
player to at most two lines: for English that means at most 1,460 px of upper-case text in total (about 95 letters), which leaves room
for the word that wraps early; translations run longer and may use up to 1,560 px. The font has no Cyrillic or CJK glyphs (the game falls
back to another font there), so a Cyrillic capital is estimated at 20 px and a full-width character at 35 px. `bridge/daily-notice-fit.test.mjs`
holds the daily-limit text of all 16 languages to that, with a three-digit and a four-digit {limit}. The daily-limit notice is the one
exception to "two lines": it carries a forced line break (`\n`) so the first sentence is line 1 and the detailed Mistral / Local AI
sentence ("Try Mistral (free key, ~5,000 a day) or Local AI (unlimited).") starts on a fresh line 2. The test holds line 1 to ONE line of the
frame (English at most 770 px of upper-case text, translations 785 px, against the 800 px wrap) and lets line 2 wrap to a second line, so
the notice is at most three rendered lines (a frame of about 139 px: 3 x 33 px of line plus 40 px of margin). It is up for 9 s after the frame
opens (about 13 s with the intro and outro), and its roughly 96 letters take about 6.5 s to read at 15 letters a second. Every language keeps
the full second sentence, with "free" in it. Longer explanations belong in Settings (the help of the rows) or Phone Chat, which has room.

How long. `PlayerPuppet:SetWarningMessage` always asks for 5 s, counted by the game after the intro (about 2.7 s) and followed by an outro of
about 0.9 s, so an ordinary notice is readable for about 6 s. A notice listed in `M.showS` (`mod/notices.lua`; today `daily-limit` and `setup-problem`, 9 s each,
about 10 s readable and 13 s with the intro and outro) is written once to the same blackboard line with that `duration` (`M.screen.timed`),
the way the loading notice is. If that write fails it falls back to the ordinary five-second notice. While it is up, the loading notice does
not cover it (it waits the duration plus the intro and outro, `M.lastSentWait`, instead of 3 s). No other notice's duration changed. The setup notice is one short paragraph of at most two lines in every language (`bridge/setup-notice-fit.test.mjs`, which shares its measure, `bridge/notice-fit.mjs`, with the daily-limit test).

## Loading notices

A switch that takes time (an OmniVoice voice after a voice change or at start, Local AI warming up for about a minute the first time) says so
instead of leaving the player wondering. The loading board in `mod/notices.lua` (`convaiNotices.loading`) keeps one entry per component.
The loading line appears when the load has lasted 1.5 s and then stays on screen as ONE notice, without blinking or repeating, until the
"ready" line (or a failure line that says what to do) replaces it. A load that finishes quickly shows nothing. Several loads at once are
one notice with a line each; when the set changes (a second load starts) the text is updated in place. Popups only show in gameplay, so an
outcome reached in a menu is shown on the first gameplay tick.

How it stays up. The game's warning line (`WarningMessageGameController`) plays an intro and a jingle for every message and hides it after the
message's own `duration`; `PlayerPuppet:SetWarningMessage` (which every other notice uses) always asks for 5 s, so a notice built on it can
only be repeated, and each repeat blinks and chimes. The loading notice is instead written once to the same blackboard line
(`UI_Notifications.WarningMessage`, a `SimpleScreenMessage` with `duration = 90`) by `M.screen.post` in `mod/notices.lua`, from CET Lua with no
redscript change, and taken down by a message with `isShown = false` (`M.screen.clear`, the game's own outro). The 90 s is a safety net: if
the mod is reloaded or stops while the notice is up it still goes away by itself. While it is held:
- it is posted again instantly (`isInstant`, no intro) every 80 s, before the game would hide it, and when a second load joins or one ends
  without an outcome line;
- a menu opening mid-load posts nothing there; when gameplay returns the notice is posted again once, instantly;
- another notice from the mod replaces it; it comes back, with its intro, 3 s later (never on a notice younger than 3 s), or 5.5 s after an
  outcome line when other loads are still going;
- a game notice (loot, encumbrance) that replaces it is waited out: the line is read once a second (`M.screen.read`) and ours is posted
  again when nothing is on screen. If that read cannot be trusted (it never shows our own text) the check is given up on for the session;
- when the load ends without an outcome line (cancelled, switched away, an outcome too old), `M.release` hides it, but only when it is still
  the notice on screen; it never hides a newer notice;
- if the blackboard line cannot be written at all, the notice falls back to the ordinary five-second notice shown again every 3 s (the
  behaviour before 2026-10-13), so it never disappears.
Cost: nothing per frame. The caller is the existing 0.25 s slow update; with nothing loading it is one comparison, and the state is read at most
once a second. While a notice is held the screen is touched only to post, change or hide it, plus one blackboard read a second.

A companion still being prepared is a line of this same notice (keys `prep1` to `prep3` in `mod/manager-ui.lua`; the lines come from the invitation
board, `waiting()` in `mod/manager-party.lua`, which reads what its last sample saw). The line's text changes while its wait goes on, so the
read may carry a `revisions` table (key to text): a changed text is posted in place, instantly, like a change of the set, and an unchanged one
never is. These lines have no ready line ("{name} joined your group." is that, an ordinary notice) and no failure line of their own: on a
failure the line is dropped and the invitation's own failure notice says what failed. What blocks her comes from the bridge's receipt (`stage`
of a memory-state row: `queued`, `memory`, `voice`; the state `connecting`; no fresh receipt means the service has not answered yet), through
`mod/invitation-readiness.lua`.
Failures, as the player reads them after "{name} could not prepare:": the conversation service did not answer for a minute (wait, invite again);
the voice model did not load (check the voice in Settings or choose another one, invite again); the memory service did not respond, or the
conversation connection did not open (check the connection, invite again). Other bridge texts are shown as they are. The shared plan's daily
limit counts conversation turns, not preparation, so it never blocks a spawn.

Status signals read, all of them already published by the bridge and read on a slow tick (never per frame; nothing is read while nothing loads
except the once-a-second check):
- OmniVoice and Pocket: `runtime/local-voice-state.json` (`workerBackend`, `requestedProvider`, `state` loading / ready), decoded by `mod/manager.lua` anyway.
  Only the engine the player chose counts, and only the model load (`loading`), not a per-character preparation (`warming`).
- Local AI: `localAi` in `runtime/mistral-state.json` (warming, ready, failed, no-memory), read only while Local is the conversation provider.
No signal exists yet for the Mistral cloud voice's reference preparation (it is a cached, sub-second step), or the voice browser's warm-up; each can add a state to `manager.loadStatus` and two lines to `loadingText` in `mod/manager-ui.lua` when it has one.

## Adding a notice

1. Add an entry to `M.catalog` in `mod/notices.lua` with its class (and a `repeatS` window for a Player notice a key press can repeat).
2. Show it with `convaiNotices.send(player, 'my-id', text)` (or `sendLines`, `permit`); never call `ConvaiPhoneFeedback` directly.
3. Add its rows to the table above. `node --test bridge/notices.test.mjs` fails on an unknown id, a raw call, a catalog entry nobody
   uses, or a row missing from this page.
4. Wording: short, friendly, no file names or internal words; add all 15 locales and run `node scripts/Extract-UiCatalog.mjs`.
