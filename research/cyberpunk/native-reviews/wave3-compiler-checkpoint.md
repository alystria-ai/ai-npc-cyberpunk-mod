## Implemented subject checks

- Genuine typed `gameJournalContact/id` binds its `name/value` LocKey to the exact reviewed English onscreen entry. Native aliases and spellings are explicit ledger receipts, including exact subject/title pointers and binary/JSON hashes.
- Every new spoken claim joins the typed native actor ID to the screenplay speaker and item, then to a typed emitted `scnDialogLineEvent`. An explicit event pointer is verified; otherwise only event pointers already cited by that claim are considered. Unused lines and choice events fail.
- Subject speakers and third-party witnesses have separate exact name/voice/source-hash receipts. A witness cannot become the subject merely by changing the attribution mode. Named contact ownership never repairs a generic world actor acquisition gap.
- New speech retains the exact en_us descriptor and payload index, actual matching variant IDs, and same-ID typed shipped English subtitle evidence. Distinct reviewed wording is retained exactly; it is never normalized into a claimed byte match.
- The actor-record validator accepts only the speaking actor active `findInContext` or enabled `spawnDespawn` typed TweakDBID field. Numeric records use exact CRC32 plus ASCII length against current roster records, ignoring runtime offset bits. Zero, ambiguous, inactive and neighboring-actor bindings fail.
- Actor-record qualification additionally requires a reviewed native displayName/fullDisplayName property receipt and exact record-specific English localization refs. A similar secondaryKey alone fails. R3n0 currently reaches this honest property-binding hold.
